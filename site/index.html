The Dock

Every window, one hover away.

Rest the mouse on an app in the Dock and its windows pop up in a list. Click one to go there. Your Dock stays exactly as it is.

  • Browser tabs, listed. Safari, Chrome, Edge, Firefox and friends show each window's tabs. Click one to jump straight to it.
  • Click to minimize, click again to restore, like the taskbar always did.
  • Minimized windows and other desktops included.
⌘Tab, Flip 3D edition

Switch windows, not apps.

Hold ⌘ and press Tab: every window stands in a slanted 3D stack, most recent in front. Windows 7 fans will feel right at home.

  • Type to filter. Type "plan" and only your plans are left. Return opens the one in front.
  • Minimized, full-screen and other-desktop windows are all in the stack.
  • Keeps working next to password fields, where lesser switchers give up.

Snapping

⌃⌥ is your Win key.

Arrows for halves, maximize and quarters, or drag a window to an edge or a corner and let go. Unplug your display and plug it back in: every window returns to where it was.

  • ⌃⌥← ⌃⌥→ halves, ⌃⌥↑ maximize
  • ⌃⌥D show desktop, ⌃⌥E new Finder window
  • Every shortcut is listed in DockFlip's menu bar menu.
Privacy

Polished without peeking.

Most window switchers ask to record your screen the moment you open them, because they're built on screenshots. DockFlip draws everything from what macOS already shares with it, so it never needs to see your screen.

  • One permission: Accessibility.
  • No networking code. No analytics, no tracking, no update pings.
  • Your typing stays yours: ordinary keys only while ⌘Tab is open.

Install

Two minutes, one detour.

Download

Open DockFlip.dmg and drag DockFlip to Applications.

Open it

macOS says it can't check the app. Press Done. Not "Move to Trash".

Open Anyway

System Settings → Privacy & Security → scroll down → Open Anyway.

Allow

DockFlip's setup opens. Switch it on under Accessibility. Done.
